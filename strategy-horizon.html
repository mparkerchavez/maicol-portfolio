01 /// The Challenge

Staying Ahead of AI's Acceleration

As AI capabilities advanced rapidly, it became harder for organizations to maintain a shared, realistic understanding of what was emerging, what was hype, and what would matter over the next 3–5 years. Without a clear narrative grounded in signals and data points, teams risked becoming reactive, chasing new developments like generative AI after they surfaced, instead of building informed readiness over time.

The Tech Trends report was designed to help senior business and technology leaders and enterprise architects track where AI and other emerging technologies were heading, so the organization could make better long-range decisions about readiness, responsible adoption, and where to focus attention. Over time, AI increasingly touched almost every trend area, making it important to frame AI's evolution in a way that was actionable across business units, not just as a standalone topic.

02 /// The Strategy

Turning Signals into an Enterprise Point of View

To help leaders stay ahead of rapid change, we treated the Tech Trends publication as an annual synthesis of signals, not a retrospective summary. I authored AI-focused sections by combining external research and industry reporting with perspectives from relationships across venture capital firms, startups, and financial services partners. The goal was to translate what we were seeing in the ecosystem into an enterprise lens: what was emerging, what was becoming practical, and what teams should pay attention to next.

I focused on two themes: how AI is changing the way people work, and what signals leaders should pay attention to in order to judge whether investments were translating into meaningful value over time. Rather than presenting a single ROI formula, I framed directional guidance that helped teams ask better questions about impact and readiness in Capital Group's context, not just in the abstract.

03 /// The Outcome

Building Shared Context for Smarter Decisions

The AI narrative work helped leaders and teams interpret rapid shifts in generative AI and emerging capabilities through an enterprise lens, so the organization could be less reactive and more deliberate about readiness and responsible use.

I delivered a "State of AI" session to a business unit group to summarize where generative AI stood, what Capital Group had explored so far, and where capabilities were heading. Beyond that, I used the research and narrative assets in a more embedded way, working directly with teams on specific use cases and translating external signals into practical implications that informed decisions about what to explore next.

This work also informed architecture conversations tied to enabling AI capabilities across the enterprise, including Microsoft Copilot Studio, helping teams align on capability direction, constraints, and what effective adoption could look like in practice.
