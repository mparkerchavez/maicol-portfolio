01 /// The Challenge

The 5-Minute Prep Problem

Capital Group's sales associates often have limited time to prepare for client meetings, sometimes just 5–10 minutes between travel and back-to-back schedules. They needed a way to quickly understand who they were meeting with, what had happened in prior conversations, what mattered most right now, and what actions would make the meeting effective.

A data science team supporting the Client Group had access to rich internal context across client databases, historical notes, sales activity, and relevant insights. But turning that raw context into a meeting-ready plan still took time, and the early experience patterns did not reliably guide associates to the most relevant information fast enough. The result: inconsistent preparation, uneven meeting quality, and missed opportunities to follow up on the right topics.

02 /// The Strategy

Validating the Workflow, Not Just the Tech.

Instead of starting with features, we ran an innovation sprint to validate where AI could remove the most friction in the sales associate workflow. I led sessions with sales associates to map their end-to-end meeting preparation journey, identify the highest-impact breakdowns, and generate multiple solution directions. While we suspected a "meeting plan" would be the right output, we used the full workflow to confirm it was the highest-leverage opportunity.

To ground decisions in real behavior, we had associates sketch what their ideal experience would look like. Those sketches, combined with the workflow mapping, informed what we prototyped and how the experience needed to fit inside an existing platform they used every day.

  • Insight 1: Sales associates did not want to "chat." They wanted a meeting-ready plan they could act on quickly.
  • Insight 2: Trust is fragile, one incorrect or low-quality output can stall adoption.

Based on what we learned, I guided a shift from open-ended exploration toward a structured meeting-prep workflow. We tested both a high-fidelity Figma prototype and a functional Streamlit prototype with real users to validate our hypotheses, assess the usefulness of the meeting plan with next-best actions, and identify where we needed to pivot. I translated those insights into workflow requirements and clear next-step build priorities for product and engineering.

03 /// The Outcome

From Months to 3–4 Weeks

By validating the workflow and outputs before full build, we accelerated the path from idea to evidence. In 3–4 weeks, we produced validated learnings that stakeholders could use to make budgeting and investment decisions with confidence, and we translated those learnings into clear build priorities for product and engineering.

That foundation enabled adoption to expand from a small pilot group to a broader rollout of 300+ sales associates in under a year. The result was faster decision-making, reduced assumption-heavy development, and a repeatable pattern for validating GenAI use cases inside an existing platform.
